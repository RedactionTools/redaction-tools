A tool's page is built from records, not prose, and every section is there to answer one
question. This page says which.

## Key facts

The panel at the top is the comparable part — the fields every listed tool has, so that
two pages can be read side by side.

| Field           | What it says                                                                                                                      |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vendor**      | Who sells it, and the country they are headquartered in. "Not recorded" means we could not establish it, not that they have none. |
| **Media**       | Which of PDF, image, video, audio and text the tool redacts.                                                                      |
| **Deployment**  | Online, desktop, self-hosted, API or browser extension. A tool can be several.                                                    |
| **Method**      | Manual, rule-based, AI-powered or hybrid — how the tool decides what to cover.                                                    |
| **Free tier**   | Yes or no. This is "free forever", never a trial.                                                                                 |
| **Trial**       | The published trial length, or "None". A trial and a free tier are recorded separately and always displayed separately.           |
| **Entry price** | The cheapest published rate, with its unit. "Not published" is a finding, not a blank.                                            |

Every one of those five — media, deployment, method, a pricing position, and our own
writing — has to be present before a tool becomes a page at all. See
[what a listing has to clear](/docs/methodology#what-a-listing-has-to-clear).

## What it does

Our own description, written by an editor. It is at least 400 characters, and vendor copy
never counts toward that minimum. If a tool has been claimed by its vendor, anything the
vendor wrote appears lower down under **From the vendor**, in its own labelled block — so
you always know who is talking.

## Plans and pricing

One row per public plan, with its price, its unit and what it includes.

Three things to watch for:

- **A metered plan usually carries two rates** — a monthly fee and an overage rate that
  applies once its allowance runs out. Both are shown, because either one alone misleads.
- **An allowance is published in the unit the vendor published it in.** A plan that caps
  pages is not described as capping documents, even where that would make the table tidier.
- **"Not published"** means the vendor does not state a figure. We do not fill it in by
  inference, and we do not treat an unpublished cap as unlimited.

Each price carries how we obtained it and when we last checked — read automatically,
entered by an editor, or supplied by the vendor. The marks and what they oblige us to are
in [the methodology](/docs/methodology#where-a-price-comes-from).

Note that the **last-checked date is not the date the price changed**. Prices are
re-checked far more often than they move, so that date describes our attention, not the
vendor's behaviour.

## Our assessment

**Strengths** and **Limitations**, written by us. These are editorial judgements and are
labelled as such. They are not scores, they are not derived from a benchmark, and no vendor
can buy, edit or veto them.

## Common questions

Shown only where we have questions worth answering for that tool. An absent section means
we had nothing to add, not that the tool is obscure.

## What a listing is not

It is not a benchmark result. Nothing on a tool's page measures how _well_ it redacts —
whether the text under a black box is really gone, whether a face is missed in frame 300.
That work is designed and scheduled but not yet published, and until it is, no figure here
should be read as a quality score.
